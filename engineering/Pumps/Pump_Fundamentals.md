Pump Fundamentals

Pump fundamentals describe how a pump moves fluid and generates head (pressure/energy) in a system. This is essential knowledge for commissioning, piping, and process engineers.

⸻

1. What a Pump Does

A pump adds energy to a fluid so it can move through a system.

Main purposes:
	•	Transport fluid
	•	Overcome system resistance
	•	Deliver required flow rate

Key parameters:

Parameter	Symbol	Description
Flow rate	Q	Volume flow (m³/h, l/s)
Head	H	Energy per unit weight (height equivalent)
Power	P	Energy consumption
Efficiency	η	Performance ratio


⸻

2. Pump Head (Most Important Concept)

Pump head represents energy added to the fluid.

H = \frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{v^2}{2g} + z

Where:
	•	p = pressure
	•	ρ = density
	•	v = velocity
	•	z = elevation

In practice:

Pump head =
	•	static height
	•		•	friction losses
	•		•	required outlet pressure

3. Main Types of Pumps

3.1 Centrifugal Pumps (Most Common)

Working principle:
	•	Rotating impeller accelerates fluid
	•	Velocity converts into pressure

Advantages:
	•	Simple design
	•	High flow rates
	•	Low maintenance

Disadvantages:
	•	Poor for high viscosity fluids
	•	Sensitive to cavitation

Applications:
	•	Cooling systems
	•	Water supply
	•	HVAC
	•	Chemical processes

⸻

3.2 Positive Displacement Pumps

Working principle:
	•	Trap a fixed volume of fluid
	•	Push it forward

Types:
	•	Gear
	•	Piston
	•	Diaphragm
	•	Screw

Advantages:
	•	Constant flow
	•	High pressure capability
	•	Good for viscous fluids

Disadvantages:
	•	More complex
	•	Require overpressure protection

4. Pump Curve

The pump curve shows the relationship between:
	•	Flow (Q)
	•	Head (H)

|
|        *
|      *
|    *
|  *
|*
+-----------------> Head (H)

👉 As flow increases → head decreases

6. NPSH and Cavitation

If inlet pressure is too low → fluid starts to vaporize.

This is called cavitation.

Symptoms:
	•	Noise (like gravel)
	•	Vibrations
	•	Performance drop
	•	Impeller damage

Key condition:

NPSHa > NPSHr

Where:
	•	NPSHa = available
	•	NPSHr = required

7. Commissioning Checklist

Typical checks:
	1.	Rotation direction
	2.	Pump priming
	3.	Suction line open
	4.	Suction/discharge pressure
	5.	Flow rate
	6.	Vibrations
	7.	Bearing temperature
	8.	No cavitation

⸻

8. Common System Mistakes

1. Undersized suction line

→ Cavitation risk

2. Too many valves/fittings

→ High pressure losses

3. Air in system

→ Pump loses performance

4. Closed discharge valve

→ Overheating

⸻

Practical Insight (Commissioning)

If a pressure gauge is fluctuating, common causes:
	•	Cavitation
	•	Air in system
	•	Unstable flow
